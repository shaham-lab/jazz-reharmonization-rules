# The rulebook: what a reharmonization agent is allowed to play

A companion page for a reinforcement-learning agent that reharmonizes jazz standards
by editing chords. The agent may only make edits that jazz theory sanctions; this page
is that vocabulary.

**→ [Open the page](https://alonyamin1.github.io/jazz-reharmonization-rules/)**

Each of the 31 entries — 29 substitution rules plus two structural moves — shows:

- what the rule does and when it fires,
- a real example found by scanning the corpus for a place the rule actually applies,
- the notation before and after, with the edited chord highlighted,
- audio of both, on one player with a BEFORE/AFTER toggle that keeps its place, so you
  can flip mid-phrase and hear the harmony change underneath,
- a cursor tracking the music across the staff as it plays.

Examples were chosen by the project's learned critic, so each rule is heard where the
move works rather than at the first place it happened to fire.

## Provenance

The rules were drawn from the standard jazz-harmony literature and from the
reharmonization lessons on thejazzpianosite.com, in collaboration with **Anton Petrov**
and **Shai Cohen**. Lead sheets come from the Wikifonia archive and the JazzMus corpus.

## A note on rights

The excerpts here are eight bars long and are presented as research commentary
alongside the analysis they illustrate. The audio is a synthesized rendering from chord
symbols, not a recording, and no full scores are distributed. This is a non-commercial
academic page.

Lead-sheet sources: the JazzMus corpus (CC BY-NC 4.0) and the Wikifonia archive.
